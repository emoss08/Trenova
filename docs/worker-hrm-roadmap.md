# Worker HRM Roadmap

Turns the Workers module from an asset record into a human-resources system. Each phase is
independently shippable and follows the house rules: GraphQL first (REST only where a file upload
or download needs it), hexagonal Go services with audited mutations, `@trenova/shared` for anything
both apps consume, RED-first tests, and the existing design system (panels, tabs, KPI tiles,
badges, dock actions) before any new component.

Status legend: `[ ]` not started · `[~]` in progress · `[x]` done

## Review findings (2026-09-03)

Phases A–F have shipped. This is what a read of them against the regulations a
carrier actually answers to, and against how the pages get used day to day,
turned up. Every box here is open. Phases M–R below carry the work, and the
order at the end of this document is the one worth following.

### Fix now

- [x] **Termination does not revoke Dash access.** `driverportalservice.RevokeAccess` existed, but the termination cascade in `workeremploymentservice` never called it, so a terminated driver kept a working portal until somebody closed the offboarding checklist item by hand. **Done 2026-09-03:** the cascade now calls a `PortalAccessRevoker` port, wired by setter because the portal service reaches back here through the safety service. It reports whether there was any access to shut off, and a failure is surfaced in the cascade summary and warned about in the UI rather than raised, because the worker row is already Inactive by the time the cascade runs. The offboarding checklist item auto-satisfies once the sign-in is unlinked.

### Legal obligations with nothing behind them

- [x] **Drug and alcohol testing (49 CFR Part 382).** *Closed by Phase N.* No test records, no random selection pool, no FMCSA Clearinghouse queries. `DispatchControl.EnforceDrugAndAlcoholCompliance` already exists, so dispatch can be told to enforce a status HR has no way to evidence.
- [x] **Driver qualification file (49 CFR 391.51).** *Closed by Phase O, except the single-PDF export.* We hold the pieces — credentials, MVR reviews, training, checklists — but never assemble them, never record the previous-employer safety performance investigation that 391.23 requires at hire, and have no retention rule for the three years after someone leaves. Phase L's "DQF completeness report" is the reporting half only.
- [x] **Injury recordkeeping (OSHA 300/300A).** *Closed by Phase P.* The safety module is FMCSA-shaped: accidents, citations, roadside inspections. Nothing models a recordable injury, lost or restricted days, or the annual log. A carrier owes both records and they are not the same record.
- [x] **FMLA entitlement.** *Closed by Phase Q.* Phase D added FMLA as a leave type, which implies the product manages it. It tracks open and closed only, not the twelve-week entitlement over a rolling year, intermittent leave, or certification deadlines.

### Gaps in what we shipped

- [x] **No overview of a person.** *Closed by Phase M.* The worker panel has fourteen tabs, each with its own roll-up, and nothing that composes them into "is this driver in good standing right now".
- [x] **The roster is blind to all of it.** *Closed by Phase M.* Worker list columns stop at name, status, tenure, type, driver type, fleet, state, compliance status and created date. Nothing for training, safety or expiring credentials, so the list cannot be filtered by anything phases A–F added.
- [x] **Everything is assigned one worker at a time.** *Closed by Phase M.* There is no way to put every OTR driver through a new course. A required-course matrix across a fleet is the missing tool.
- [x] **Driver notifications pile up.** *Closed by Phase R.* Each phase added its own kind. A driver can get credential, training, review and discipline notices in the same week with no digest.
- [x] **Safety has no fleet view.** *Closed by Phase R.* The scorecard is per worker. No ranking, no trend, and the points scale is homegrown rather than mapped to the CSA BASIC categories a safety director reasons in.

## Phase G — Scheduling & availability

**Done 2026-09-05.** Four tables (`shift_templates`, `worker_shift_assignments`, `worker_availability_preferences`, `shift_swap_requests`), one service, a Scheduling page under HR, a Schedule tab on the worker panel, and a schedule card in Dash.

**The load-bearing decision: the rota is derived on every read and never stored.** A stored week would be wrong within the hour — time off gets approved, a leave case opens, dispatch puts a load on a Saturday — and keeping it right would mean invalidating it from five other services. Composing it from the evidence instead makes the board correct by construction, and the cost is four grouped queries a week rather than a walk of the roster.

- [x] Shift/rotation templates; weekly rota merging PTO, leave and dispatch assignments. A pattern is a **seven-character day mask indexed from Sunday** rather than an array, so it reads at a glance and compares without unpacking, and a pattern with **no working days is refused** — it is a way to roster somebody onto nothing and never notice. An **A/B rotation is one template with a two-week cycle and two assignments at different offsets**, not two near-identical templates. The rotation is resolved **once a week, from the week start**: the seven-day buckets counted from the epoch begin on a Thursday, so a per-day answer flips midweek and puts both halves of an A/B pair on at once — caught by its own test before it ever ran. States are ranked so **the strongest fact wins** (Leave > TimeOff > Assigned > Unavailable > Scheduled > Off): a driver on approved leave never shows as rostered, but the load dispatch already put on that day is still reported, because the dispatcher needs to know work is sitting on a day nobody is covering. A **conflict** is a day the pattern rosters somebody onto that they cannot work — time off on a day they were never rostered is not a hole in the roster, which is what stops every weekend reading as a conflict for anybody on leave. Time off and leave come back from the repository **as the ranges they are stored as** and are expanded into days by the composer: expanding them in SQL would need `generate_series`, which is Postgres-only, and a week is seven days. Moving somebody onto a new pattern **ends the one in force the day before**, so a worker is never on two at once, and retiring a pattern is refused while anybody is still on it.
- [x] Availability preferences and swap requests from Dash. A preference is **a statement, never a constraint** — dispatch is free to override it, and the rota carries it through even when something stronger decided the state, so the override is visible rather than silent. A swap needs **two acceptances, the colleague's and the office's**: a swap the office never saw is a shift nobody is covering, so `Proposed → Accepted → Approved` cannot be short-circuited. The driver-facing half enforces **who is answering** — only the counterparty accepts or declines, only the requester withdraws, and approving is not reachable from the app at all. `outgoing` is computed on the server, because a client that had to work out which side of a swap it was on would first have to be told its own worker id. The Dash card is offered **only once the driver is actually on a shift**: an empty rota reads as a roster nobody filled in rather than as a job with no fixed pattern, which is what an owner-operator has.
- [x] Tests. Domain: the day mask, the shift with no working days, the end minute past midnight, the swap state machine and its terminal states, the swap with yourself, the decision with no date, and the rota composer — the pattern followed, the strongest fact winning, leave outranking time off, time off on an unrostered day not counting as a conflict, the preference carried through an override, and the A/B rotation alternating rather than doubling up. Service: the prior assignment ended the day before, the assignment starting later refused rather than written backwards, the offset beyond the cycle, the retired shift, retiring a shift people are on, the swap state machine, the decision stamped, and the three actor rules. Rota: open-ended leave covering the rest of the week, an A/B pair alternating across a two-week board, and a load on a day off counted as a conflict. Client: the mask both ways, the clock wrapping past midnight, the shift window and its next-day marker, the pattern description collapsing runs, the week start mirroring the server, the week summary excluding hours nobody is working, and the swap actions each side is actually allowed.

## Phase H — Compensation history & benefits

**Done 2026-09-04.** Two tables (`benefit_plans`, `worker_benefit_enrollments`), five columns on the existing recurring earning and deduction tables, one service, a Benefits page under HR, a benefits section on the worker Pay tab and a total-compensation card in Dash.

**The load-bearing decision: an employee contribution is taken through an ordinary recurring deduction rather than a second mechanism.** The settlement builder already knows how to take money off a settlement — how to cap it, pause it, order it, and reverse it when a settlement is voided. A parallel benefits deduction would have to be taught all of that again and would drift from the original within a release. The same reasoning makes a garnishment an ordinary deduction too, and a bonus an ordinary earning.

- [x] Pay-rate change history, bonuses, garnishments with court-order references. **`worker_pay_assignments` already was the rate history** — effective-dated, with rate overrides — and `EmploymentEventRateChanged` already existed on the timeline, so this phase surfaces them rather than rebuilding them. A **bonus** is an earning whose cap equals its amount: it pays once and completes itself down the path everything else uses, and the new `kind` is what lets a screen offer it as a bonus instead of making somebody set the cap by hand. A **garnishment** is a deduction carrying the order it is taken under, and it is **refused without one** — money taken from somebody's pay with no reference to the authorising order cannot be defended. Deductions now carry a `priority` and are **taken in priority order**, which is what makes a court order outrank a voluntary deduction when pay will not cover them all; creation order remains the tie-break, which is all the old query sorted by.
- [x] Benefits enrollment feeding settlement deductions; total-compensation statement in Dash. Each plan year is its own row and the price is **copied onto the enrollment**, so repricing a plan next year cannot silently restate what somebody was charged this year. A **waiver still produces an enrollment** — "declined" and "nobody asked" are different facts and only one of them is a problem at audit — and it takes nothing and costs nothing, since carrying the plan's price on it would show up in the employer's total as cover nobody has. Coverage tiers scale the employee-only price as a starting point that a carrier pricing each tier separately can override. Ending cover ends the contribution with it, setting the deduction's end date as well as its status so a settlement generated for an earlier period still sees it as live for that period. The **total-compensation statement adds the employer's share and never the employee's** — money the worker paid is not money the job gave them — and it composes pay from the settlement area rather than recomputing it, which is what stops two answers to "what did this driver earn".
- [x] Tests. Domain: the pay-code requirement, the plan that has to cost somebody something, the employer-paid plan that is still valid, tier scaling including the free plan, the waiver reason, the backwards window, which statuses deduct, and the garnishment refused without its order. Service: the enrollment opening an ordinary uncapped deduction at benefit priority, tier scaling and the explicit override, the waiver opening no deduction and carrying no cost, the archived plan refused, ending cover stopping the contribution and dating it, and the total adding only the employer share and ignoring a waiver. Client: money rendered from minor units, the annual and share arithmetic, and the Dash card leading with the package rather than the pay and keeping the driver's own contribution out of it.

## Phase I — Org structure & manager workflows

**Done 2026-09-04.** Two tables (`job_positions`, `approval_delegations`), one service, an Org Structure page and a My Team page under HR, and a new `team` data scope that narrows an existing grant to the people a manager actually manages.

- [x] Positions/job titles, headcount by fleet/terminal, manager hierarchy ("my team"). **`workers.manager_id` already existed** — it has been on the table since the worker migration and the Transfer employment event already moves it — so the phase added the index that makes reading a manager's team cheap, not the column. A position is what somebody does, a fleet code is where they do it, and headcount is read both ways plus by department; the three groupings come back in one shape so the page lays them out identically, and the totals are taken off the terminal grouping because it is the one that sums to the roster. My Team reaches people two ways — the workers who name you, and the terminals you run — and **keeps them apart on the page**, because a terminal manager covering forty drivers is not forty direct reports. A position reporting to itself is refused in the domain and the whole chain is walked in the service, since a cycle in an org chart is an infinite loop for anything that draws it. Archiving a position is refused while anybody still holds it.
- [x] Delegated approval chains for PTO, expenses, timesheets. A delegation names a scope — everything, time off, or expenses — because a manager handing a specific queue to a specialist must not hand over the rest by accident, and it is **kept rather than deleted when revoked** so an approval made under it can still be explained. The key decision: **a delegation widens what the delegate may act on, never what the delegator could approve in the first place**, so delegating from somebody who manages nobody hands over nothing — correct rather than a bug. Enforcement is a new `DataScopeTeam` sitting between `own` and `organization`: the permission engine answers whether the grant exists and how wide it is and deliberately knows nothing about who manages whom, so the narrowing happens at the resolver where the worker being acted on is already known. Every wider scope is untouched, so an HR administrator approving for the whole company keeps working exactly as before. A decision taken through a delegation records whose authority it used. Timesheets are Phase J and do not exist yet; the scope enum and the resolver helper both take them without changing shape.
- [x] Tests. Domain: the self-reporting refusal, department validation, code normalisation, scope coverage, the self-delegation and backwards-window refusals, and the four states of a delegation window including a revocation winning over it. Service: acting for your own people, refusing somebody else's, reaching through a delegation and reporting whose authority was used, a delegation of the wrong scope not answering, a revoked or expired one not answering, one from a manager of nobody handing over nothing, and that the authorization question costs exactly one check rather than a listing of the whole team. Client: the two team paths kept apart in both the list and the counts, the attention count covering all three ways somebody can need chasing, the delegation window states, and the headcount share arithmetic.

## Phase J — Time & attendance

**Done 2026-09-05.** Three tables (`time_clock_entries`, `timesheets`, `payroll_exports`), one service, and a Time & Attendance page under HR with a clock, an approval queue and payroll runs.

**The load-bearing decision: a timesheet's totals are frozen when it is submitted rather than recomputed on read.** Everywhere else in this build a roll-up is derived, because a stored one goes stale. Here the roll-up *is* the decision — it is what a manager approved and what payroll was paid from — and recomputing it later would quietly change a signed-off wage record, which is the one thing a wage record must never do. While a week is open it is re-rolled from its punches on every write; from submit onward the numbers are the record.

- [x] Clock in/out and daily timesheets for non-driving staff, manager approval, payroll export. **Only employees are paid by the clock** — a contractor invoices, and recording their hours would produce a wage record for somebody who is not owed wages. A worker is **on the clock once or not at all**: a second open punch is refused by the service and made impossible by a partial unique index, because two open entries is somebody being paid twice for the same hour. A punch **cannot be dated into the future**, an entry **cannot be longer than a day** (that is a forgotten clock-out, not a shift worked straight through), a break comes out of a closed period and never a running one, and an open entry is **worth nothing yet** so no roll-up depends on when it was read. Weeks start on the **same Sunday the rota starts on**, so a timesheet and a rota row line up without anybody converting between them; the sheet is opened by insert-on-conflict so two punches landing at once cannot both create it. Overtime is everything past a threshold **copied onto the sheet** rather than read from a setting, so changing the rule next quarter cannot restate an approved week; **paid leave sits outside the split** — time nobody worked does not earn a premium — and is read from approved time off *overlapping* the week (a week off that starts on the Friday before still covers this Monday), converted at one standard day per day off, so a sheet cannot say somebody was here when the same system says they were signed off. Every manual entry and correction **carries who changed it and why**, and is refused once the week is submitted; a corrected entry that moves weeks re-rolls both. A worker hands their own week over and nothing else; an empty week cannot be submitted, because a zero-hour payroll line for somebody who was simply away is a line somebody has to explain; a resubmission clears the old approval rather than leaving it to read as a sign-off on numbers that changed. A payroll run **locks the weeks it carried in one statement** and stamps them with the run, so a period cannot be sent twice without somebody voiding the first — and voiding needs a reason, keeps the run rather than deleting it, and puts the weeks back to Approved, not Open, because the hours were signed off and still are. The CSV goes out in decimal hours with ISO days, quoting anything with a comma in it, because the thing on the other end is a payroll system somebody imports into by hand.
- [x] Tests. Domain: paid minutes with and without a break, the open entry worth nothing, the backwards entry, the day-long entry, the break that swallows the entry, the running entry with a break, the state machine including the reopen and the void, editability, the overtime split with and without a threshold, paid leave outside the split, the week with more hours than a week has, the undated approval, the backwards period, and the void without a reason. Service: the contractor refused, the second punch, the future punch, the week opened on the right Sunday, clocking out with nobody on the clock, the roll-up on clock-out, the correction without a reason, the correction after submit, the deletion without a reason, the state machine, the empty week, the totals frozen at submit and left alone afterwards, the worker approving their own week, the worker submitting somebody else's, the resubmission clearing the old approval, the empty payroll period, the run locking what it carried and totalling it, the void without a reason, the void reopening the weeks, and approved leave overlapping the week counted by the days actually inside it. Client: hours rendered with the part hour kept, the running clock never going negative, which actions each viewer gets on each status, and the payroll file's header, decimal hours and quoting.

## Phase K — Self-service in Dash

**Done 2026-09-05.** Three tables (`worker_policies`, `worker_policy_acknowledgements`, `worker_profile_change_requests`), one Dash-control toggle, one service, a Policies page under HR, two new sections on the worker Portal tab, and a Policies card plus an approval-aware contact editor in Dash. Credential renewal upload and training completion **already existed** (`uploadMyCredentialDocument`, `acknowledgeMyTraining`) and were left alone.

**Two decisions carry the phase.** An acknowledgement **copies what was signed** — the version label and the document's SHA-256 — because the policy row will move on and the signature must not; so **changing the words under a signed policy without changing its version is refused** (a title or summary change is not words anybody signed and goes through). And a change request stores **before-and-after pairs rather than a copy of the wanted record**, so a manager sees exactly what is being asked and an approval applies exactly that — never a field outside the closed list, whatever the stored changes say.

- [x] Profile edits with approval. A new `requireContactChangeApproval` toggle on Dash control (off by default, so every existing carrier keeps the direct edit they rely on) turns a driver's contact edit into a request: the profile comes back unchanged, the app says "sent for approval" before the tap rather than after, and the pending request sits beside the details it would change with a withdraw button. Only the **closed list of contact fields** can be asked for — licence, medical card and endorsements stay carrier-controlled, because a driver who could edit their own qualifications could also edit them into compliance. A request that **changes nothing is refused** rather than queued, a **second request while one waits is refused** (a manager deciding two versions of the same address will approve the wrong one), and a **rejection needs a reason**. The office decides it from the worker's Portal tab under the new `profile_change_request` resource, team-scoped through the same `requireTeamScope` gate as time off; an approval writes the changes through the worker service so every existing hook fires, and the driver is told either way through a new `dash.profile_change_reviewed` notification kind that branches on the decision.
- [x] Policy/handbook acknowledgement with e-signature. A policy is **either text or an attached document, never neither** — a signature has to be on something — and it names its audience, because a handbook for employees is not a contract term for an owner-operator and a driver is never shown a policy that does not bind them. Signing is a **typed full name that has to match the name on the record** (case and spacing forgiven, "ok" refused), an explicit "I have read this", and the request's **IP and user agent** — carried through a new `gqlctx.ClientInfo` the GraphQL handler now stamps — kept with the moment and the checksum: a record that a specific person, from a specific place, at a specific time, agreed to a specific text. Signing the same version twice returns the first signature rather than a second row. **Who has signed is derived on read** by left-joining the audience against the version in force, so a new hire is outstanding the moment they are hired and a new version puts everybody back to outstanding; the Policies page shows the roll-up, the worker Portal tab lists every version they ever signed with superseded ones marked. The Dash card sits at the top of the profile while anything is outstanding and drops to the bottom once it is not.
- [x] Credential renewal upload and training completion — already built in Phases A and E and reused as-is.
- [x] Tests. Domain: audience coverage, the policy with nothing to sign, the attached document as enough, which fields count as the words that were signed, the signed-versus-read receipt, the diff listing only what moves in address order, the unchanged snapshot as no request, the request that changes nothing, the field outside the closed list, the rejection without a reason, the approval applying only allowed fields, and the state machine's finality. ## Phase N — Drug & alcohol testing

**Done 2026-09-04.** Five tables (`worker_dot_tests`, `worker_dot_violations`, `worker_clearinghouse_queries`, `dot_random_pools`, `dot_random_draws` + entries), one service, one GraphQL surface, a Testing tab on the worker panel and a Random Testing console under HR.

- [x] Test records (pre-employment, random, post-accident, reasonable suspicion, return-to-duty, follow-up) with result, collection site, MRO verification and document. **One row per substance analysed**: a collection covering both is two rows, because only the drug half has a medical review officer and only the alcohol half has a concentration, and a schema that let either cross over would hold numbers the analysis never produced. An alcohol reading grades itself — 0.04 and above is filed as positive whatever the clerk chose — so a 0.11 cannot be entered as negative.
- [x] Random selection pool with a periodic draw against the DOT minimum rates, and evidence of how the draw was made. The draw is `HMAC-SHA256(seed:substance, workerId)` sorted ascending: unpredictable before the seed exists, and reproducible forever after, so an auditor with the seed and the roster gets the same names in the same order. Per-round targets round **up** — taking 12 of a 12.5 target every quarter would finish the year at 48% and under the minimum. Drug and alcohol draw independently; being picked for one does not exclude anybody from the other.
- [x] FMCSA Clearinghouse: pre-employment full query, annual limited query, violation and return-to-duty status on the worker. - [x] Wire `DispatchControl.EnforceDrugAndAlcoholCompliance` to a real status so a prohibited driver cannot be dispatched. `worker_profiles.drug_alcohol_status` is the roll-up the three tables write and the roster, the dispatch check and the panel all read. **Only a standing prohibition blocks**, and it blocks at every enforcement level: missing paperwork warns instead, because grounding a fleet over a gap in the file is not what 49 CFR 382 asks for. ## Phase P — Injury & OSHA recordkeeping

**Done 2026-09-04.** Two tables, one service, an Injuries section on the worker Safety tab and an OSHA 300 Log page under HR. **The organisation is the establishment** — it carries one address, which is what OSHA means by one — so no establishment table was invented; a multi-terminal carrier already models each terminal as its own organisation.

- [x] Injury and illness records with recordability, body part, treatment, lost and restricted days, and a link to the safety event where there is one. **Every case is kept, recordable or not**: the decision *not* to record one is itself worth a record, and first aid alone is explicitly not recordable (29 CFR 1904.7(b)(5)(ii)) — that distinction is what the whole log turns on. Case numbers restart each calendar year and are never reused, because an auditor reading a log with two case 4s cannot tell them apart. The classification is *suggested* from treatment and day counts and then stops moving once somebody makes the judgement themselves; recordability is the employer's call, but it should not have to be made from a blank field. Day counts cap at 180 (1904.7(b)(3)(viii)), and a case with days away cannot be filed in a lesser column and vanish from the count that matters.
- [x] OSHA 300 log and 300A annual summary per establishment, carrying the electronic-submission fields. **The summary's case totals are not stored** — they are counted from the log on every read, because 29 CFR 1904.33 requires the log be corrected for five years and a stored total would go stale the first time somebody did. Certification records who signed and when, and is **refused while cases are still accruing days**: certifying a log that has not finished moving is certifying a number about to change. Correcting a certified summary means reopening it first, which clears the signature — a signature on figures that have since changed is worse than none. TRIR and DART are null until hours are recorded, because a rate with no denominator is not a small number; it is not a number.
- [x] Workers' compensation claim reference and status on the record, with filed and closed dates guarded so a claim nobody filed carries no dates.
- [x] Permission: a new `worker_injury` resource at **Restricted** sensitivity, granted separately from the rest of a worker's record — the log carries body parts, treatment and claims, and most people who need the roster have no business reading it. Somebody without the grant sees no section at all rather than an empty one implying there is nothing there.

## Phase Q — FMLA & extended leave

**Done 2026-09-04.** Three tables (`leave_controls`, `worker_leave_cases`, `worker_leave_entries`), one service, a Leave tab on the worker panel, a Leave Settings page under HR, a standing strip on the employment timeline and a leave card in Dash. Reuses the existing `LeaveType` and the `LeaveStarted`/`LeaveEnded` employment events rather than inventing a parallel notion of being away.

- [x] Entitlement tracking: twelve weeks over the rolling year the organisation chooses, in the unit it chooses. **Derived on read, never stored** — under the rolling method the window moves every day, so a stored balance would be wrong by tomorrow. All four methods in 29 CFR 825.200(b) are implemented; the rolling window ends inclusively (today's leave counts against "the twelve months up to today") while the three fixed periods end exclusively. Weeks are converted to hours through a configurable workweek, because leave is granted in weeks and taken in hours. Remaining is floored at zero: designating leave after the fact can push usage past the entitlement, and a negative balance would read as time owed. Eligibility is split — tenure is answered from the hire date, the 1,250 hours cannot be (there is no timeclock here) and is recorded by hand on each case.
- [x] Intermittent leave drawing on the same entitlement, with certification requests and their deadlines. Days are recorded in **hours** (29 CFR 825.205) against a case, so continuous and intermittent leave draw on one balance with no second mechanism. `countsAgainstEntitlement` is **copied onto the entry when the day is recorded**, so undesignating a case later cannot silently rewrite what was already counted. Approving and designating are separate decisions (825.301): leave can be approved as company time without being charged to FMLA, and the tab offers the two separately rather than conflating them. Lateness is derived from the deadline rather than swept, so a case becomes overdue on its own.
- [x] Leave balance visible to the worker in Dash and to HR on the timeline. Dash gets `myLeave` — a trimmed projection that leaves the office's notes and the decision-maker in the office — behind a `leaveBalance` feature that is on only once the driver has a case, because an empty FMLA card reads as time they are owed. HR gets a standing strip above the employment timeline, where a leave of absence is already an event and the balance is the next question anybody asks. The home tile carries the organisation-wide count of certifications still owed: past the deadline the employer may deny the leave (825.313), so it is a queue, not a statistic.
- [x] Leave Settings under HR for the measurement method, the entitlement, the workweek and the eligibility figures, with the trade-off of each method stated where it is chosen — the rolling method is the only one that cannot be stacked into twenty-four consecutive weeks, which is why most employers pick it.

## Phase R — Fleet safety & notification hygiene

**Done 2026-09-04.** One new table (`worker_safety_violations`), two columns on `dash_controls`, seven grouped queries, a Fleet Safety page under HR, a violations list on each inspection or citation, and a digest pass on the nightly compliance sweep.

- [x] Fleet safety view: ranking, trend over time, events by kind and by terminal. **Seven grouped queries, not a walk of the roster** — every number on the page is an aggregate, and pulling a few thousand drivers into memory to count them is the wrong shape at any fleet size. The ranking and the terminal breakdown read the roster cache (`worker_profiles.safety_rating`/`safety_score`) that the roster list itself shows, so the two cannot disagree; active points are the exception, summed live because they roll off on a date rather than on a write. Best and worst are the same query read from opposite ends, so a driver cannot top both. The trend buckets by UTC month in SQL — months are what a safety meeting talks in — and reports a direction only from four points or more, because two points are not a trend.
- [x] Map event kinds onto CSA BASIC categories so the scorecard reads the way a safety director thinks. **A violation is a row, not a column.** A single roadside inspection routinely cites several violations in different BASICs, and a schema that allowed one would throw away most of what the inspection said. Each row carries the FMCSA severity weight (1–10, bounded at the API edge so a weight nobody could look up never reaches the record) and an out-of-service flag, kept beside the weight rather than folded into it so the number stays readable against the published tables. Scoring is the agency's own: severity, plus two for out of service, times three inside six months, twice inside a year, once out to the twenty-four month look-back, and nothing beyond it. A carrier who records events but keys no violation codes still gets a scorecard, **inferred from the kind of event and labelled as such** — an estimate somebody knows is an estimate beats a blank page, and beats a false number by more. A clean inspection belongs to no BASIC: filing it under maintenance would make a fleet that passes look like one that fails. Bars are relative to the fleet's own worst category and deliberately not called a percentile — the FMCSA ranks a carrier against its peers, and this data cannot.
- [x] A daily or weekly driver digest so one week of obligations arrives as one notice. `DashControl.driverDigestCadence` is **Immediate by default**, so no carrier silently loses notices they already rely on; Daily and Weekly bundle instead, and the per-obligation reminders are suppressed while a round-up is in force so nobody is told twice. The digest is its own activity walking the same lists rather than sharing state with the reminder passes: Temporal activities are separate invocations and may not run on the same worker, so a bucket filled in one and read in another would be empty often enough to be a bug nobody could reproduce. A weekly notice looks a fortnight ahead so nothing falls due in the gap between two of them, and fires only on its configured weekday. The correlation key is the period — the date, or the ISO week — which is what makes a retried activity idempotent and tomorrow's run a new notice. A digest carrying something already lapsed is raised to high priority: a round-up of next week is not the same message as one saying a licence expired on Tuesday. Turning reminders off is refused with the digest on, rather than quietly collecting obligations and sending nothing.
- [x] Tests. Domain: the BASIC suggestion including the clean inspection that belongs to none, the three recency weights and the cliff past twenty-four months, out-of-service adding two before the multiplier, the weight and BASIC validation, folding recorded and inferred figures together, all seven returned in the agency's order even at zero, and the average that gives an empty fleet no score. Service: the roster summary and header totals, window and rank-limit clamping, ranking from both ends, terminal averages. Jobs: the cadence gate including the weekly weekday and the reminders-off case, the horizon per cadence, the period key's idempotence within an ISO week, and the priority lift on a lapsed obligation. Client: the seven BASICs, the inferred banner, the rankings, the terminal breakdown, the empty trend, and the relative bar arithmetic.
